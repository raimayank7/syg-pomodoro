How to choose

The right rhythm depends on the work more than on the person. Ask two questions. First: how long does this work take to get into? Revising flash cards takes seconds; picking up a half-written program can take a quarter of an hour. The longer the warm-up, the longer the round should be, so that the warm-up is a small share of it. Second: how hard is it to start? For a task you dread, a short promise is easier to keep, so choose the shortest round.

That gives a simple guide. Use 25 and 5 for revision, routine practice, small tasks and anything you are avoiding. Use 50 and 10 for writing, coding and long problems. Use 45 and 15 when you plan your day in clock hours, as most school students do. Keep 90 and 20 for one or two sessions a day on your hardest problem.

What stays the same

Whatever the lengths, the rules of the method do not change: one named task per round, no switching, stop at the bell, and take the break away from the screen. A longer round is not a looser one. And the proportion of rest matters: when you lengthen the work, lengthen the break with it. Skipping breaks to "save time" is the surest way to run out of attention by mid-afternoon.

Give a rhythm a fair trial

Changing the lengths every day tells you nothing, because every day differs for other reasons too. Pick one rhythm and keep it for a week. Each evening, note the rounds completed and how the work felt. Then try another for a week and compare. The timer on this site records your daily counts for the last seven days in your browser, which makes the comparison easy.

Mixing rhythms in one day

Many people settle on a mixture: a long session or two in the morning for the hardest work, and short rounds in the afternoon for lighter tasks. That is sensible. Decide the pattern in advance, when you plan the day, and not in the moment when a short round simply feels like less effort. The day planner works with any lengths you type in.

Questions people ask

Which timer should I start with?

Start with 25 and 5 and keep it for a week or two. Change to a longer rhythm only if the bell keeps cutting you off in work that needs a long warm-up.

Can I set my own lengths?

Yes. On the home page timer, open Settings and type any focus length from 1 to 180 minutes and any break length. Your choice is remembered in your browser.

Does the timer keep running if I switch tabs or lock the phone?

Yes. The time is worked out from the clock, so it stays correct. With the bell and notification switched on you are told when the round ends; some phones silence web pages when the screen is locked, so keep the screen on if you rely on the bell.
