When the Pomodoro method does not work: problems and fixes

When the method fails, the cause is nearly always one of a dozen ordinary things, and each has an ordinary fix.

1. "I start the timer and then stare at the page."

The task is too vague. "Study biology" gives the mind nothing to do. Rewrite it as an action with a result: "draw and label the heart from memory, then check". See splitting tasks.

2. "I keep picking up the phone."

The phone is too near. Settings help, but distance helps more: another room, a closed bag, behind you. Read phone distraction.

3. "The bell breaks my concentration."

Write one line about what comes next before you rise; you will be back in the task within a minute. If that is not enough, use longer rounds such as 50 and 10. More on the deep work page.

4. "My five-minute break turns into forty."

The break has a screen in it. Change the break to something physical that ends by itself: fill the bottle, walk to the gate, stretch. Time the break and stand up when it rings. See breaks.

5. "I do three rounds and then I am finished for the day."

Usually one of three things: the breaks were skipped or spent on a screen, the long break was missed, or the first rounds were on too little sleep and food. Keep the breaks, eat properly, and build the number of rounds slowly over weeks.

6. "People keep interrupting me."

Tell them the next time you will be free, note the matter, and go to them when the round ends. Choose quieter hours for your hardest work. The exact steps are on the interruptions page.

7. "I feel guilty when I do not reach my target."

The target was a wish, not a plan. Count what you really completed this week and set next week's target just above it. The record is for learning, not for punishment.

8. "I spend more time planning than working."

Cut the system down to three things: today's short list, an estimate beside each task, a mark for each round. Five minutes in the morning, two at night. Colour codes and applications can wait.

9. "My tasks never fit 25 minutes."

They do not need to. A big task takes several rounds; a small one shares a round with others. The round measures your attention, not the task.

10. "It works for study but not for my job."

A job with meetings and calls has room for fewer rounds. Protect two to six rounds for work that needs thought and let the rest of the day be what it is. See pomodoro at work.

11. "I cannot sit even for 25 minutes."

Begin with 10 or 15 minutes and add a few minutes each week. A short round completed is better than a long one abandoned. For children this is the normal starting point; see the timer for children.

12. "I was doing well and then stopped using it."

That is the most common story of all. Habits lapse during holidays, illness and busy weeks. Restart with one round tomorrow morning on one small task. You do not need to rebuild the whole system on the first day.

When the method is not the answer

A timer cannot fix a workload that is simply too large, a subject you lack the basics for, or a place where study is impossible. Those need other answers: saying no, going back to an earlier chapter, finding a library. And if concentration has been very difficult for a long time and in every part of life, or low mood and worry are in the way, a doctor or counsellor is the right person to see. The method is a useful tool for ordinary distraction; it is not a treatment for anything.

Some work does not need it

Meetings, teaching, physical work, caring for someone, creative play: not everything should be timed. Use rounds where they help, which is mostly solitary work that needs thought, and leave the rest of life unmeasured.

Questions people ask

How long before the method becomes a habit?

Give it two honest weeks with the same rules each day. By then you will know your usual number of rounds and whether the classic length suits you.

Is it all right to change the rules?

Yes, after you have tried the standard form. Change one thing at a time, such as round length, and keep it for a week before judging.

What is the single most useful fix?

For most people: put the phone out of reach and write a clear task before pressing Start.
