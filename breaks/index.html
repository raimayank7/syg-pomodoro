What to do in a pomodoro break

The break decides the quality of the next round. Five good minutes are worth more than fifteen spent scrolling.

What a break is for

A break has one job: to let your attention let go of the task, so that you can pick it up fresh. Anything that gives the mind a new load of information does the opposite. This is why a break spent on short videos or a busy chat group leaves you more tired than before. You have not rested; you have changed the thing you are concentrating on.

Good five-minute breaks

  • Stand up and walk. To the balcony, the gate, the water cooler, another floor.
  • Drink water or make tea.
  • Look far away. Out of a window at something distant. After a long time at reading distance, the eyes welcome it.
  • Stretch the neck, shoulders, wrists and back gently.
  • Do one small physical chore: fold clothes, water a plant, clear the desk, fill the bottle.
  • Breathe slowly for a minute with eyes closed.
  • Step outside for light and air if you can.

The common thread is simple: away from the screen, moving a little, nothing new to think about.

Breaks that cost you the next round

  • Social media, reels and short videos. They are built to keep you, and five minutes becomes twenty.
  • News and match scores, which leave thoughts behind.
  • Starting a conversation that cannot end in five minutes.
  • Email or work chat, which hands you a new problem just before you return to the old one.
  • A heavy snack in every break.

If you must check the phone, do it in the long break, and decide before you unlock it what you are going to look at.

The long break

After four rounds, take 15 to 30 minutes. This is the time for a meal, a real walk, a bath, a short talk with family, or lying down with eyes closed. A short rest of ten to twenty minutes with an alarm can help in the afternoon; a long sleep usually makes the restart harder. Do not use the long break to squeeze in another task. Four rounds are about two hours of concentration, and the long break is what makes a second and third set possible.

Start and stop on time

Use the timer for breaks as well as for work. The timer on this site moves to the break by itself and rings when it ends. The dangerous moment is the end of the break: the mind offers "two more minutes". Stand up and return at the ring, even if you only sit and look at the task. Starting is most of the battle; once you write one line, the round is under way.

When you do not feel like a break

Sometimes the work is flowing and the bell feels like an interruption. Take a shorter break, but do take it: stand, stretch, sip water, and note in one line what you were about to do next. That line lets you restart in seconds. If you regularly find 25 minutes too short for your kind of work, move to longer rounds such as 50 and 10 instead of skipping breaks.

Breaks for the eyes

Screen work and close reading keep the eyes fixed at one distance for long periods. A habit of looking at something far away during every break is easy to keep and feels good. If your eyes are often sore, dry or strained, that is a matter for an eye doctor, not for a timer.

Children and breaks

For children, the break should include movement: a run to the door and back, ten jumps, a quick game of catch. A child who has moved will sit again. See the timer for children's homework.

Questions people ask

Can I skip the break if I feel fresh?

Take at least a short one. Breaks work by prevention: you take them before you feel tired, so that the fourth and eighth rounds are as good as the first.

Is five minutes enough?

For most people, between two rounds, yes. If you return still foggy, try seven or eight minutes, or check whether your break is really a rest or only a different screen.

What should I do in a long break at the office?

Leave the desk. Walk outside the building if possible, eat away from the screen, or talk to a colleague about something other than work.
