      A rhythm that fits the clock

      The appeal of 45 and 15 is that it is easy to live with. A round and its break together take exactly one hour, so a timetable can be written in plain clock hours: Maths from 5 to 6, Science from 6 to 7, English from 7 to 8. You start every round on the hour, and you always know where you are in the evening without looking at a chart. Many school periods run to about this length, so the stretch feels familiar to students.

      Who it suits

      • School students who plan by subject and by hour.
      • Families, because a parent can see at a glance when the next break falls and plan meals around it.
      • Tuition and coaching homework, where each subject gets one clean hour.
      • People who find 25 minutes too short but 50 minutes with only a 10 minute break too tiring.

      The longer break is the special feature. Fifteen minutes is enough for a snack, a short walk outside, or a few minutes of play, which makes this rhythm kinder for younger students and for long evenings after a full school day.

      The cost

      You pay for the comfort with a lower share of working time. In each hour, three quarters is work and one quarter is rest. With 25 and 5 or 50 and 10, five sixths is work. Over a four hour evening, that is 3 hours of study against 3 hours 20 minutes. For most students the difference is small, and it is easily repaid if the more generous break keeps them going for an extra hour.

      How to use it well

      Give each 45 minute round one subject and a definite job, such as one exercise or one set of pages, and spend the last five to ten minutes of the round with the book closed, writing down what you remember. Do not let the 15 minute break stretch: time it, and avoid starting a show or a game that cannot end in a quarter of an hour. After three or four rounds, take a longer rest for dinner.

      The day planner will build a timetable in this rhythm: enter 45 for the focus length and 15 for the short break. For how to choose what goes into each round, read the guide for students.

      Questions people ask

      Is a 15 minute break too long?

      Not if it ends on time. It is long enough for a snack or some movement. Time the break and avoid screens that are hard to leave.

      Can I use 45/15 for college or office work?

      Yes. It suits any day planned in clock hours. If you want more working time per hour, 50 and 10 is the nearest alternative.
