How to stop putting work off and start

You do not need to feel ready. You need to work on the thing for 25 minutes. Feeling ready usually arrives about ten minutes in.

Why we put things off

We rarely delay because we are lazy. We delay because the task feels unpleasant in some way: too big, unclear, boring, or likely to show that we are not as good as we hoped. Putting it off removes that feeling for the moment, which is why the habit is so sticky. The cure is not a lecture to yourself. It is to make the start so small that the unpleasant feeling has nothing to hold on to.

Promise one round, not the whole task

"Write the project report" is frightening. "Work on the report for 25 minutes and then stop" is not. Make that promise and keep it exactly: when the timer rings you are free to stop. Very often you will not want to, because the hard part was the beginning. But the permission to stop must be real, or the trick fails the next time.

Find the smallest first step

Ask: what is the first physical action? Not "study chemistry" but "open the notebook to the page on the mole concept and copy the first worked example". Not "do the accounts" but "collect this month's bills in one pile". If you still do not start, the step is not small enough yet. Make it almost silly: open the file and type the heading. Small starts are not cheating; they are how large things begin.

Decide when and where in advance

A task with no time attached is always for "later". Give it an appointment: "At 4:30, at the desk, I will start one round of the report." Write it down. Decisions made in advance are easier to follow than decisions made in the moment, when every excuse is at hand. The day planner produces exactly this kind of timetable.

Clear the path

  • Put everything the task needs on the desk the night before.
  • Close what it does not need: tabs, chat, the phone. See phone distraction.
  • If the task is unclear, make the first round a planning round: write what is asked, what you already have and what the steps are.
  • If the task is boring, pair it with a pleasant setting: good tea, a clean desk, music without words.

Do the avoided task first

The task you are avoiding drains you all day while it sits undone. Give it the first round of the day. Even one round changes it from "not started" to "started", and a started task is much easier to return to. On the days you manage only one round on it, that is still one more than yesterday.

When you are stuck inside a round

Sometimes you start and then sit frozen. Lower the bar: write a bad first version, solve the easiest question first, make a list of what you do not understand. Movement of any kind breaks the freeze. You can improve a rough draft; you cannot improve a blank page.

Be fair to yourself

Harsh self-talk feels like discipline but usually leads to more avoidance, because it makes the task feel even worse. Note what happened, plainly: "I planned four rounds and did one." Then ask what made the other three hard and change one thing tomorrow. If putting things off is constant and is harming your studies, work or peace of mind over a long period, speak to a counsellor or doctor; a timer is a tool, not a treatment.

Questions people ask

What if I cannot even start one round?

Make the first step smaller and shorten the round. Ten minutes on the timer is a fair start on a bad day. The aim is to begin, not to impress anyone.

Why do I work well only at the last minute?

A near deadline removes the choice to delay, so you start. A timed round creates a small, near deadline every 25 minutes, which gives some of the same push without the panic.

Does rewarding myself help?

A small pleasant thing after a set of rounds, such as tea, a walk or an episode in the evening, can help. Keep it after the work, not before.
